How these numbers are worked out

Contribution = revenue you keep − product − shipping − packaging and handling − card processing

Break-even ROAS = AOV ÷ contribution and Target ROAS = AOV ÷ (contribution − target margin × revenue you keep)

Store ROAS = total sales ÷ total ad spend. Also called MER or blended ROAS. Because every order carries the same contribution, it is judged against the same break-even and target ROAS as part one.

Cost per new customer = total ad spend ÷ new customers. It is usually higher than break-even CPA, because it loads all ad spend onto first-time buyers only. Returning orders are how that cost is earned back.

Cost to acquire a purchase = total ad spend ÷ orders, taken from last month in part two, so it counts every order the store took rather than only those Ads Manager tracked.

Sales from returning customers = total sales × returning customer rate. An estimate, since returning customers can order more or less than new ones.

Profit after ads = total sales × (contribution ÷ AOV) − total ad spend. If your total sales figure already has refunds taken off, this slightly understates profit.

Sales not credited to any source are the gap between total sales and what Meta, Google and organic channels each claim. Ads drive part of that gap, through brand searches, direct visits and word of mouth, and part would have happened anyway. When ad platforms claim more than you sold, they are counting the same order more than once.

Sales Meta likely helped win are an estimate, not tracked results. Someone who saw an ad and later searched the brand on Google, typed the web address, or clicked an email had Meta as one touchpoint along the way. The presets give Meta this share of each group: Cautious 10% of Google Ads sales, 5% of organic and 20% of uncredited sales; Balanced 20%, 10% and 35%; Generous 30%, 20% and 50%. Some of these buyers never saw an ad at all, which is why no preset gives Meta everything. A holdout or conversion lift test measures this properly.

Tax sitting inside your prices is taken out of revenue but still counted in ROAS, because ad platforms and store totals both see the full amount the customer paid. Refunded orders lose their revenue, the product goes back into stock, and delivery, packaging and card fees are not recovered.
